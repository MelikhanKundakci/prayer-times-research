# Notification readiness is a separate claim

This repository is a calculation research preview. **No exported method is currently certified for unattended prayer-start notifications everywhere it accepts input.** `productionReady: false` in each method's `validation.json` is intentional. A close match to a displayed calendar minute is evidence about one publisher's output at the tested points; it does not establish the underlying religious rule, a precise physical sky event, or the event's UTC date.

The app's primary direction is [local calculations under documented rules](LOCAL-VALIDATION.md). It should enable automatic alerts only for an explicitly supported combination of method version, event, location domain, date range and timezone semantics after that combination passes a separate release review. A method name alone is not a coverage claim. Other calculations can remain visible as research estimates, with manual choice or local mosque schedules where appropriate. Reproducing a city's timetable is a separate claim and is not required merely to calculate a user's point under an independently specified rule.

## Evidence needed before an alert profile is enabled

For each proposed profile, keep a review record containing:

1. **Meaning and authority:** the named prayer-start convention, Asr school where relevant, high-latitude rule, Ramadan or lunar-calendar dependency, and whether the reference is an institution's calendar or a software approximation. Agreement with a publisher is not an endorsement by that publisher.
2. **Inputs:** the calculation point or geographic zone, elevation/horizon assumptions, applicable IANA timezone, and allowed range of dates. For a local profile, validate the supplied user point and timezone; an institution's production coordinates are required only for claims that depend on reproducing its city output. An input accepted by the mathematical routine is not automatically in the reviewed domain.
3. **An absolute event:** the UTC instant and its local civil date, not just `HH:mm`. Validate day assignment independently around midnight, year boundaries, DST gaps and folds, and UTC offsets at the event instant. For publisher-reproduction claims, also establish the source row's date meaning. Missing date metadata in a publisher's calendar does not invalidate a separately verified local physical instant.
4. **Missing and exceptional events:** explicit handling for absent twilight, polar day/night, nonphysical shadow markers, ambiguous `00:00` cells, and order failures. Do not turn a diagnostic or an arbitrary substituted time into a prayer-start alert.
5. **Independent accuracy evidence:** for local profiles, separately verify the astronomical equations, dated event instants and documented rule choices. Use independent astronomical references with compatible conventions, test known exceptions and quantify model limits. Cover seasons, DST, different relevant points and years, including future dates. Numerical self-consistency does not establish observed accuracy. For claims about publisher agreement, additionally freeze the algorithm before new complete calendar comparisons and report expected, comparable, missing and ambiguous slots, error distributions, date errors and regressions under the [calendar protocol](VALIDATION.md). A zero-error sample is always bounded by its scope.
6. **Operational update path:** pin the timezone database and any lunar-calendar rules used offline. Recompute pending UTC alarms after a location, chosen profile, system timezone, legal timezone data or relevant lunar announcement changes. A cached wall clock must never be converted to an alarm using a different offset without recalculation.

An alert profile is blocked if any needed event lacks a defensible UTC date, if a high-latitude replacement is required but unspecified, or if a known model/rule error exceeds the reviewed tolerance inside the proposed domain. A fitted city/date correction cannot establish a missing rule. A calendar discrepancy at a different point or under a different stated convention is not automatically an error in the local calculation. The review can be scoped more narrowly instead of forcing one worldwide pass/fail result.

## Current examples of the boundary

| Profile or study | What the evidence supports | What still blocks broad alerts |
|---|---|---|
| Local `diyanet-published-point-v1` | Independent continuous-model verification plus source-attributed angles, shadow criterion and margins. A declared annual guard admits bounded ordinary northern twilight; its local night/frame conventions are explicit. Actual event dates and unresolved statuses are returned. | Northern seasonal/horizon selection is incomplete; terrain, observer height and variable atmosphere are not modeled. Cross-night scheduling and mobile alarm delivery need their own review. See the [local validation contract](LOCAL-VALIDATION.md). |
| Moonsighting Committee V4.2 | Two new complete city-years matched all 5,117 displayed fields after the formula and timezone bundle were frozen. The public code returns UTC instants. | Original calendars expose only clocks, not event-specific UTC dates. The known sample has source DST disagreements in Sydney and Wellington, a Kiritimati date-row issue, and unreviewed polar replacement semantics. These exceptions must be investigated by domain; source clocks cannot override legal local time. |
| Local `local-northern-seasonal-v1` | Explicit opt-in summer policy; annual point geometry, night fractions, transition endpoints and dated cross-night ordering can be verified independently. Estimated events retain provenance. | Project-defined seasonal conventions, not official Diyanet reconstruction or a religious acceptance finding. Short/polar horizons remain unsupported; mobile delivery and model adequacy remain separate. See [the rule](../core/local/SEASONAL.md). |
| Diyanet northern seasonal | Three new 2027 calendars are within one minute only under a **conditional** next-day-Isha reading. The selected northern missing-window route now reports 17 raw Dhuhr→Asr reversals across 28 known city-years without changing a time. | Across the broader known set, 77 source-row Isha date conflicts and eight `00:00` cells remain unresolved. A model ordering check cannot establish the publisher's date meaning or turn the conditional score into an alert-ready contract. See the [chronology audit](../methods/diyanet/NOTIFICATION-READINESS.md). |
| Umm al-Qura own reconstruction | Five of six fresh annual point calendars were entirely within one displayed minute; the full six-year comparison has 61 two-minute Cape Town values. | The source accepts a fixed numeric zone and supplies no event-specific UTC dates. Lunar-month and high-latitude policies need confirmation. Sydney fixed UTC+10 evidence is not Sydney legal-DST evidence. |
| ARC compatibility | A separate opt-in model improves publisher-clock agreement on three fresh annual calendars. A later eleven-year offset diagnostic brings every comparable displayed clock within one minute. | The offset is an unconfirmed source-display hypothesis, not a physical event-time rule. Midnight assignments need an independent date source. |
| Türkiye Takvimi | A published Temkin table gives a coherent, testable numeric lead. | Sunrise and Maghrib remain systematically wrong in the current Istanbul reconstruction. A [direct optical-horizon replacement](../methods/turkiye-takvimi/HORIZON-COMPONENTS.md) worsened the full-year comparison and was rejected. Do not use either diagnostic for alerts. |

See the [validation protocol](VALIDATION.md) and [accuracy priorities](ACCURACY-PRIORITIES.md) for denominators and other families. This table is an engineering release assessment of the current research evidence, not a ranking of religious practices. It should be updated when new blinded, independently sourced comparisons or confirmed institutional rules change the picture.

The [legal-DST regression](../tests/moonsighting-legal-dst.test.mjs) protects all seven Moonsighting event UTC instants and local dates around the Sydney 2027 and Auckland/Wellington 2028 transitions. It follows the [New South Wales rule](https://www.nsw.gov.au/about-nsw/daylight-saving/) and [New Zealand's daylight-saving order](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/regulation/public/2007/0185/latest/whole.html): the offset changes on Sunday. It deliberately does not adjust our UTC event to match a publisher table that changes its clock on Saturday.
