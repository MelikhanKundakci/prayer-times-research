# Further Diyanet diagnosis: minute residuals and source gaps

This is a **post-exposure analysis** of existing reference calendars. It changes no numerical module, creates no new institutional validation cohort, and is not an accuracy claim for future locations.

The existing northern missing-window experiment has 45,200 exact matches out of 52,554 comparable fields over 24 known city-years under its conditional Isha date interpretation; 52,509 are within one minute. Of the 45 remaining two-minute differences, 38 occur in autumn transitions, with 29 in Narvik 2027 and ten Frankfurt Fajr 2027. In the separately frozen three-city 2027 cohort obtained later, 6,145/6,568 are exact and all comparable fields are within one minute. Two Bergen `00:00` Isha strings remain unresolved.

A one-second global event shift raises the **known** exact count from 45,200 to 45,336, but lowers the later three-city cohort from 6,145 to 6,089 and introduces three more differences over one minute in the known set. We therefore did not change the published algorithm. City-specific shifts are likewise retrospective fits to unknown production points; they are not credible rules for an arbitrary user location. A separate rounding-threshold study on ten already-known low-latitude city-years tells the same story: a 0.6-minute threshold improves Mexico City and New Delhi from 3,443 to 3,836 exact of 4,380, but lowers the eight Turkish development years from 17,051 to 15,421 exact of 17,520. All of these diagnostic figures use previously exposed calendars.

[Diyanet's published Temkin rule](https://vakithesaplama.diyanet.gov.tr/temkin.php) gives the minute adjustments, while the [high-latitude criteria](https://www.awqatsalah.com/sub/34/tespit-kriterleri) describe angles, minimum day/night length, and the broad seasonal procedure. They do not uniquely specify the interpolation equation, second-level rounding, operational location, or the event date of after-midnight Isha. The earlier previous-night and earliest-Isha experiments also had regressions and cannot be relabeled as verified fixes.

The [official Awqat API guide](https://awqatsalah.diyanet.gov.tr/files/56d83ac4-f7f5-4f6e-9b9e-b1ffeebf1b6a.pdf) shows separate `astronomicalSunrise`, `astronomicalSunset`, `sunrise` and `maghrib` fields. With authorized access, a small, predeclared comparison of those fields could test whether the northern night-ratio calculation uses raw horizon times or Temkin-adjusted clock values. Its published `CityDetail` example does not provide latitude and longitude, and the API requires registration. No authenticated data was accessed for this note.

The most useful next experiment is to predeclare one global interpretation of the autumn transition and source-night definition, compare it against all known years, then freeze complete forecasts for a **new** city or year before acquiring its calendar. Report strict printed-date and conditional Isha results side by side, including missing values and city-level regressions.

Historical evidence identifiers are `research/diyanet-improvement-2026-09-25/diagnosis/` and `research/diyanet-improvement-2026-09-25/low-latitude/rounding-study.json`. These are internal provenance identifiers rather than bundled calendars or reproducible accuracy fixtures.
