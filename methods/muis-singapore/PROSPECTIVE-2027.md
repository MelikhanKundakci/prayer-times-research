# MUIS 2027: frozen local forecast against a later official timetable

The existing, offline USNO-continuous Singapore candidate was frozen on 24 September 2026 using the already known 2024–2026 MUIS data. On 25 September, we saved its **complete 2027 forecast before opening any 2027 prayer rows**. The forecast SHA-256 is `d333c10657d68811eec998a28101c23c7d260f51ecc77d890a2c095d92309112`; the unchanged own-model source SHA-256 is `a71d578b73f6d340dc79e9c9bbec244bae51cdb97fc7ca829684e57fb74ddc51`. The runtime was Node 26.7.0, ICU 78.3, pinned IANA tzdb 2026d. This is a prospective **publisher-minute comparison**, not an observation of the sky or proof of MUIS's private calculation procedure.

The [MUIS public listing](https://www.muis.gov.sg/resources/islamic-calendar/) subsequently exposed its [2027 prayer timetable PDF](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/48/01b5692f-0f2c-44cb-b2bb-dbae0d4c4583/Prayer%20Timetable%202027.pdf) through normal access. Earlier research had received HTTP 403 for that resource; that earlier access result remains part of the history, but it no longer describes this successful later retrieval. We extracted exactly 365 dated rows, checked every expected civil date and weekday, converted each displayed 12-hour event to a 24-hour minute, and compared all six events. No 2027 row was omitted. Publisher rows are retained outside this public snapshot; their extracted private file SHA-256 is `7b0523ebc33f5652553bb5fdf9268da636b5b8ca8e8e70163d909974171a9305`.

The ten two-minute baseline differences are **all Maghrib, 8–17 February 2027**. Each of the other five events is within one minute for every day. The diagnostic changes only Maghrib on the 30 Ramadan dates, **8 February–9 March 2027**, as given by [MUIS's separately published 2027 Islamic calendar](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/48/58bf9c81-404a-4475-b0b3-5075480abfae/Islamic%20Calendar%202027M.pdf). On those 30 cells it makes 20 exact and improves the remaining ten from two minutes early to one minute early. It leaves all other 2,160 cells unchanged. For example, the official Maghrib value rises from 19:21 on 7 February to 19:23 on 8 February; the baseline is exact on 7 February and two minutes early on 8 February. On 10 March, the published value drops from 19:20 to 19:18 across the Ramadan boundary, and the unadjusted baseline is exact again. These discontinuities are evidence for a calendar-specific publication convention, but the size and rationale of any MUIS adjustment are **not documented by the sources cited here**.

The +1-minute hypothesis was suggested by exposed 2024–2026 residuals, so those years remain development data. Replaying it there yields 4,679 / 6,576 exact versus the frozen baseline's 4,608; 6,571 / 6,576 within one minute versus 6,556. It improves 87 Ramadan Maghrib cells and worsens one (17 March 2024, exact to one minute late). Five September 2025 Asr cells still differ by two minutes. Do not pool these development counts with the prospective 2027 result or describe the offset as an official rule.

The public calculator remains the unchanged local astronomical candidate. This diagnostic is **not installed as a default or a notification rule**: producing MUIS Ramadan dates for arbitrary future years offline requires a separately validated lunar-calendar policy, and MUIS may revise prepublished dates or timetable values. Even with this diagnostic, 639 of 2,190 fresh displayed minutes do not match exactly. Future work needs an institutional computation specification or new independently obtained years, with the forecast frozen before seeing each source. The comparison has zero hosting/API cost at calculation time.

The public [aggregate evidence](PROSPECTIVE-2027.json) includes each event's exact/one-minute count, the signed-error distribution, full denominators, provenance hashes, and the before/after regression totals. It contains no publisher calendar rows.
