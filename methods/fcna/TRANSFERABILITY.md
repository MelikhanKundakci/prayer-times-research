# What transfers from FCNA 15°/15° guidance—and what does not

**Finding:** FCNA publishes a transferable angle recommendation for Fajr and Isha: 15°/15° in the United States, throughout the year (and 13°/13° in Canada). That is evidence for choosing an angle profile, not a complete transferable timetable algorithm. No public FCNA source reviewed here identifies solar ephemeris, time argument, horizon convention, coordinates, rounding, or a fixed safety offset for reproducing an institution’s displayed clock. The 2025 Roseville PDF independently prints “North America” with 15°/15°, but does not establish FCNA approval or disclose those implementation details.

## The 68/246 result

On the year- and method-confirmed July–October 2025 Roseville data, the unchanged generic USA 15°/15° candidate (continuous USNO, nearest-minute display at the frozen Census address proxy) has 106/246 exact Fajr/Isha minutes and 178/246 within one minute. Its 68 differences beyond one minute are all **Isha two minutes early**; Fajr is within one minute for all 123 dates. This is a publisher-clock mismatch at one proxy point, not evidence that the FCNA angle recommendation is incorrect. The calendar itself confirms 15°/15°, but the actual production point, ephemeris and rounding are unknown; July/August print ZIP 95747 while September/October print 95678.

The Roseville-only UTC-12/ceil experiment is therefore compatibility evidence, not a general FCNA calculation rule. It improved the already exposed March/June 2024 source from 70/122 to 90/122 exact and all cells stayed within one minute. On the separate July–October 2025 table it improves overall exactness from 106/246 to 195/246, mostly by matching Isha, while Fajr exactness falls from 102/123 to 92/123. The source never says it uses UTC-12 or ceiling. On the November/December pages, where the printed header says 2024 but weekday patterns indicate 2025, the fixed variant falls from 73/122 to 69/122 exact under a 2025 assumption. The Ramadan 2026 schedule has no 15° method label and calls the morning value “Suhoor Ends”; it is not a Fajr reference and its Isha is only an unconfirmed local compatibility comparison. These mixed event, season and source-quality results do not support transferring that fixed epoch/rounding recipe to other FCNA-labeled publishers.

## Source reading

- FCNA’s [2021 method recommendation](https://fiqhcouncil.org/the-suggested-calculation-method-for-fajr-and-isha/) states the country-specific 15°/15° and 13°/13° recommendations “throughout the year”; it describes observation-based caution as recommended rather than a fixed minute requirement.
- The later FCNA-hosted [Fifteen or Eighteen Degrees article](https://fiqhcouncil.org/fifteen-or-eighteen-degrees-calculating-prayer-fasting-times-in-islam/) discusses angle uncertainty and season, latitude, altitude, obstruction and observer effects. Read with the short recommendation, this supports a general angle-selection profile while warning against treating one computed timetable as precise across locations.
- The [Roseville annual 2025 primary PDF](https://www.ispchome.com/downloads/Updated%20Annual%20Prayer%20Times%202025.pdf) prints separate Fajr Start/Isha Start columns, the 15°/15° method, and the stated Roseville postal codes. It is a publisher source for those rows, not an FCNA computation specification.

No code, angle, point, offset, rounding default or public output was changed. The pre-existing detailed annual and same-publisher diagnostics remain in [README.md](README.md) and [`validation.json`](validation.json).
